This document defines the durable strategic foundation of the established Navet brand. It is not
a list of current features and it does not introduce a new tagline or positioning.

For current provider support, feature depth, counts, and installation paths, use the
[integration capability matrix](https://docs.navet.app/integrations/), current product, and release documentation.

## Foundation at a Glance

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| Name | **Navet**, Swedish for **the hub** |
| Category | Self-hosted smart-home dashboard |
| Established public descriptor | **A smart home dashboard for every screen.** |
| Product role | A focused daily control surface for the smart-home platforms people already use |
| Primary audience | People who operate self-hosted smart homes and want a calmer interface across household screens |
| Character | Calm, deliberate, warm, clear, open, and practical |
| Enduring ideas | Room-first control, local ownership, screen adaptability, honest provider support, and open-source participation |

The public descriptor is established copy, not a prompt to generate alternate taglines. Use it when
a headline-level descriptor is needed; use a factual product description when more context is
required.

## Name and Meaning

**Navet** is pronounced **NAH-vet**. It is the definite form of the Swedish word *nav*: **the
hub**, the central point that connects a system.

The meaning explains the name and the hub mark. It should not dominate routine product copy. Tell
the origin once when it adds context; do not turn every headline into a wheel, spoke, center, or
connection metaphor.

Write **Navet** with an initial capital in prose. Lowercase `navet` is appropriate in URLs, package
names, commands, identifiers, and other code contexts.

## Purpose

Navet exists to make the connected home easier to understand and control in everyday life.

That purpose is expressed through a room-first interface, glanceable live state, direct household
actions, and consistent behavior across the screens already in a home. It is not expressed through
more configuration, more decoration, or a claim to replace the underlying smart-home platform.

## Promise

Navet gives people a calm, focused daily surface for the home while keeping their provider as the
source of truth and keeping control of the setup in their hands.

The promise has four parts:

1. **Understand the home quickly.** Important state and exceptions are visible before secondary
   detail.
2. **Act without friction.** Common household controls are direct, tactile, and close at hand.
3. **Stay familiar across screens.** The interface adapts from wall panels to phones without
   becoming a different product.
4. **Keep ownership.** Navet is local-first, self-hosted, and open source; claims about data and
   credentials must remain precise.

## Positioning

Navet is a self-hosted smart-home dashboard for people who want everyday control to feel calmer and
more coherent than an administration-first interface or a collection of separately maintained
panels.

It connects to supported smart-home platforms and presents their available capabilities through a
shared, room-first experience. The connected provider continues to run the home. Navet makes that
setup easier to use.

This positioning deliberately avoids three claims:

- Navet is not the automation platform or device source of truth.
- Navet does not promise identical capabilities across providers.
- Navet is not a managed cloud service.

## Audience

### Primary audience

People who already operate a self-hosted smart home and want a polished daily interface for wall
panels, tablets, desktops, or phones. This includes the person maintaining the system and the other
people in the household who should not need to understand its administration model.

Navet is designed to help them:

- find controls by room and purpose instead of navigating technical entity structures
- check live household state in a few seconds
- give different household screens a consistent interaction model
- reduce the maintenance burden of a collection of custom dashboards
- keep the dashboard and smart-home data under their control

### Secondary audience

Contributors, provider maintainers, designers, and developers who want to improve an open,
provider-neutral smart-home interface without making one provider's implementation the entire
product architecture.

### Not the promise

Navet is not positioned for someone who wants a fully managed cloud service, expects every advanced
feature on every provider, or does not want to operate a self-hosted application. State those limits
plainly instead of stretching the brand to fit every possible user.

## The Human Tension

Smart-home systems can be powerful for the person who configures them and tiring for the people who
only want to use the home. Default interfaces often serve administration and everyday control at
the same time. Fully custom dashboards can feel personal but require continued design and
maintenance.

Navet resolves that tension by being opinionated about the daily layer: room-first organization,
clear live state, familiar controls, and a consistent visual grammar. The emotional outcome is not
novelty. It is the quiet confidence that the home can be understood and controlled without a
manual.

## Enduring Differentiation

### Room-first, not admin-first

Navet organizes everyday control around places and household purposes. Technical provider detail is
available when it helps, but it does not lead the experience.

### One interaction model across household screens

Navet is designed to remain familiar from a shared wall display to a personal phone. Responsive
behavior changes composition and density, not the identity of the product.

### Local ownership

Navet is built for self-hosted smart homes. It does not require a Navet cloud account, and the
product promise is that provider data, dashboard state, and credentials remain on the user's own
device or server rather than Navet-operated servers. Public website analytics are a separate
surface and must not be confused with this product-data promise.

### Provider choice without false parity

Navet has a provider-neutral product direction, but provider capabilities can differ. Shared UI
should feel coherent while public copy remains specific about what each supported integration can
do today.

### Open source without unfinished-product language

Open source means people can inspect, self-host, contribute to, and help shape Navet. It is not an
excuse for rough presentation, speculative capability claims, or maintainer jargon in household
surfaces.

## Brand Principles

These are internal decision principles, not public slogans.

### 1. State before spectacle

Show what is happening in the home before adding atmosphere or explanation. Decoration supports
hierarchy; it never competes with live state or the primary action.

### 2. Household language before system language

Say **Kitchen island**, **No alerts**, or **Nothing playing** when that is what a person needs to
know. Reserve entity IDs, payloads, adapters, and capability vocabulary for technical contexts.

### 3. Direct control with visible agency

The common action should be obvious. Loading, connection, and error states should show what is
happening and provide a safe recovery or cancellation path when waiting may be prolonged.

### 4. Warmth through restraint

Navet is welcoming because it is clear, tactile, and grounded in the home. It does not need
exclamation marks, mascots, forced jokes, excessive color, or constant motion to feel human.

### 5. Consistency without sameness

Marketing, docs, and product cards have different jobs. They share geometry, hierarchy, tone, and
care while adapting composition to their context.

### 6. Honest capability over broad appeal

State what is supported, where, and under which conditions. A narrower accurate claim strengthens
the brand more than a universal claim that the product cannot keep.

### 7. Product proof over abstract promise

Use the real dashboard, live demo, current documentation, and open code as evidence. Do not replace
the product with generic technology imagery, invented testimonials, or unverified metrics.

### 8. Accessibility and performance are identity

Readable contrast, comfortable touch targets, keyboard focus, reduced motion, responsive density,
and restrained effects are part of how Navet feels dependable. They are not optional polish.

## Personality

| Trait | Navet is | Navet is not |
|---|---|---|
| Calm | Composed, focused, and easy to scan | Empty, vague, or passive |
| Deliberate | Precise in hierarchy, spacing, state, and wording | Ornamental or precious |
| Warm | At home in a real household; tactile and approachable | Cute, jokey, or overfamiliar |
| Clear | Direct about outcomes, actions, limits, and recovery | Blunt, simplistic, or full of jargon |
| Open | Transparent about code, direction, support, and gaps | Speculative, unfinished, or careless |
| Practical | Grounded in real screens, real state, and daily use | Abstract, trend-led, or feature-theatrical |

When traits compete, clarity and trust win. For example, an error may become more direct without
becoming alarming, and a security message may become more precise without becoming cold.

## Brand Architecture

### Master brand

**Navet** is the product and the master brand. The website, demo, docs, Storybook, packages, and
official deployment forms are surfaces of Navet, not separate consumer brands.

Use descriptive labels when a distinction matters:

- Navet website
- Navet demo
- Navet documentation or Navet docs
- Navet Storybook
- Navet add-on
- Navet custom panel
- Navet standalone
- Navet Dev for the established development release line

Do not invent names such as **Navet Cloud**, **Navet Home**, **Navet OS**, **Navet Pro**, or provider
edition sub-brands without an explicit product and brand decision.

### Providers

Home Assistant, Homey, openHAB, and any future providers are integrations with Navet. Use their
official names and marks according to their policies. Provider marks indicate compatibility or a
connection path; they do not replace the Navet identity or imply endorsement.

Prefer:

- **Navet for a Home Assistant setup** in explanatory prose
- **Connect Home Assistant** for an action
- **Home Assistant integration** for a capability or implementation

Avoid using **Home Assistant dashboard** as Navet's product category or implying that Navet belongs
to a provider.

### Community work and forks

Community contributions can remain part of the Navet project. Independently distributed forks must
follow the [trademark policy](https://docs.navet.app/brand/trademark/) and should use their own product identity rather
than presenting a modified distribution as official Navet.

## Message Pillars

The pillars remain stable; their supporting product facts must be refreshed.

| Pillar | Enduring message | Suitable evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday control | Find what matters by room, understand the state, and act without admin-screen clutter | Real cards, dashboard flows, and demo tasks |
| Every screen | Keep a familiar Navet experience across shared and personal screens | Current wall-panel, tablet, desktop, and phone captures |
| Local ownership | Run Navet yourself and keep smart-home data and credentials on your device or server | Architecture, security guidance, and verified data flows |
| Provider choice | Use a supported platform through a coherent dashboard without pretending capability parity | Current capability matrix and installation guides |
| Open participation | Inspect the code, self-host it, contribute, and follow what comes next | Repository, contribution guide, changelog, and roadmap |

## Proof Standard

The brand is strongest when it shows the product. Approved forms of proof include:

- current, unmodified product UI and task-based demonstrations
- the public demo, documentation, Storybook, and repository
- a dated capability matrix or release note
- reproducible technical behavior and documented architecture
- verified, permissioned customer words or metrics when those sources eventually exist

There are currently no repository-verified customer testimonials, customer logos, business
outcomes, or adoption metrics that should be treated as standing brand proof. Do not invent or
approximate them. GitHub activity and stars may be shown as live community signals when clearly
labeled; they are not evidence of a customer outcome.

## What Changes and What Does Not

| Durable foundation | Changing product fact |
|---|---|
| Navet is room-first | The rooms, sections, and card types available in a release |
| Navet is made for every household screen | The exact supported breakpoints, devices, and screenshots |
| Navet is local-first and self-hosted | The current storage paths, deployment modes, and provider auth flows |
| Navet supports provider choice honestly | The provider list and capability depth |
| Navet is open source | The roadmap, contributor count, issue state, and release cadence |
| Navet feels calm, tactile, and direct | The exact page composition, animation, and component implementation |

Update a changing fact at its source. Change a durable foundation only through explicit brand
review.

## Foundation Test

Before approving a major expression, ask:

- Does it make the real home easier to understand or control?
- Does it feel grounded in Navet's current product rather than a generic technology brand?
- Is the main user outcome or action clearer than the decoration?
- Could it belong to the website, docs, or product without contradicting the other two?
- Are local-first, provider, security, and roadmap claims precise and current?
- Does it preserve the established name, marks, color role, typography, and product character?

If any answer is no, revise the expression rather than expanding the brand to justify it.
